# Weekly lineup setting

Read this when the user asks to set a lineup or about a player's status, on every
practice-report day of a week, and on lock morning. Skill section 8 holds the rules; this
page holds the lock mechanics, the arrangement, the game-day pass, the conditionals it
carries and the week.

Two things decide a week. The projections decide WHO starts. The ARRANGEMENT of those
starters across the slots decides whether the roster can still react to news that breaks
after the first kickoff, and it is free: no projection changes when a starter moves from one
slot he is eligible for to another.

## What locks, and when

Every rostered player locks in place at the scheduled kickoff of his OWN game, starter or
bench alike. The lineup is not frozen at the week's first game; it locks slot by slot as the
windows pass.

| State at the moment the news arrives | What can still be done |
| --- | --- |
| Starter, his game has not kicked off | benched, dropped, or moved to another slot he is eligible for |
| Starter, his game has kicked off | nothing until the week ends: he cannot be benched, moved or dropped, and his points count |
| Bench player, his game has not kicked off | moved into any slot he is eligible for, or dropped |
| Bench player, his game has kicked off | never a substitute; still droppable where the host's setting for locking benched players is off |
| Free agent, his game has not kicked off | a free add, so the pool is a pivot source too |
| Free agent, his game has kicked off | on waivers until the weekly run, on a host whose weekly-waiver setting places unclaimed players on waivers at kickoff (skill section 8's date rule) |

Read the host's benched-player lock setting and its weekly-waiver setting once per league
and record both in the private document (skill section 9).

Inactive lists come out about ninety minutes before each kickoff, so a questionable player
in a later window resolves AFTER the earlier windows have locked. That ordering is the whole
reason the arrangement below matters.

## The lock order

The lineup's projected total does not depend on which eligible slot a starter occupies. Its
ability to absorb late news does. One ordered rule arranges the starters, and the kickoff
window is an input to it, never a rule of its own. The flex is filled from the position
whose starters outnumber its fixed slots.

1. When one of that position's starters is questionable, he sits in the flex and the healthy
   ones in the fixed slots: a scratch is then one move, any bench body of any flex-eligible
   position kicking off in his window or later into the flex, and it does not depend on a
   healthy starter still being unlocked; this claim outranks the latest-kickoff rule and the
   earliest-game rule. When two of them are questionable, the flex goes to the one with the
   later kickoff, since his news lands last, and the other sits in a fixed slot under (3).
2. Otherwise the flex holds the healthy one with the latest kickoff, and the week's earliest
   game never sits in the flex.
3. A questionable starter in a fixed slot is covered only by a bench body at his own position
   kicking off in his window or later, or, when the flex holder shares his position and is
   still unlocked when the news lands, by the chain: the flex holder into the fixed slot, an
   unlocked bench body into the flex; a starter neither covers takes the no-cover call.

Clause 1 comes first because the flex is the only slot a body of any eligible position can
enter, and a body enters it only while it is unlocked. With the questionable starter sitting
there, the flex stays unlocked until his own kickoff, which is after his news lands, and his
pivot needs nobody else to be unlocked. Spending the flex on a healthy starter's later
kickoff buys nothing, since there is no news to absorb on a player nobody has flagged, and
it turns the flagged starter's cover into the two-move chain, which fails the moment the
flex holder locks. A questionable starter at a position with no surplus stays in his fixed
slot: moved into the flex he would leave that slot with nobody eligible to fill it.

The chain has a price when both of the surplus position's starters are questionable: run
for the earlier one, it moves the later one out of the flex into the fixed slot, where only
a same-position body covers him. The bench body is then named for both risks (below), and
the user hears which one he is being kept for.

## The arrangement, worked

One roster with one fixed slot at position A, one fixed slot at position B, and one flex
that accepts A, B or C. Three shapes, each with the arrangement the rule yields, every slot
and every starter's pivot stated. A pivot's window is the kickoff it is good until: the
EARLIER of the pivot body's kickoff and the kickoff of the player leaving the slot, since the
leaving player locks in place at his own game.

**(a) A surplus position with one questionable starter.** Two B starters, one healthy in
the last game of the week and one questionable in the late-afternoon window; a healthy A
starter in the early window; a bench C body in the last game. No other A or B body on the
roster.

| Player | Position | His window | His slot | His pivot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| questionable B starter | B | late afternoon | the FLEX: B fills the flex and he is its questionable starter (clause 1) | the C body into the flex, one move, good until the late-afternoon window kicks off (his own lock; the C body stays free until the last game) |
| healthy B starter | B | last game | the fixed B slot, though his is the latest kickoff (clause 1 outranks the latest-kickoff rule) | none: no B body on the bench, and the flex holder, though his position, has locked by the time his news would land, so the chain is closed; he is not flagged, so no call is due |
| healthy A starter | A | early | the fixed A slot | none: no A body on the bench, and the flex holder is not his position; not flagged, so no call is due |
| bench C body | C | last game | the bench, named as the questionable B starter's pivot | |

Under clause 2 alone the healthy B starter would hold the flex and the questionable one the
fixed B slot, and his scratch would take the chain: two moves, the healthy B starter from the
flex into the fixed slot and the C body into the flex, good only while the flex holder is
unlocked. Here he plays the last game, so the chain would have held; had he played the same
late-afternoon window, both B starters would lock together and the questionable one would
have had no pivot at all.

**(b) A questionable starter with no surplus at his position, in the week's earliest game.**
A questionable A starter in the earliest game, and no other A body on the roster; two
healthy B starters, one in the late-afternoon window and one in the last game; a bench C
body in the last game.

| Player | Position | His window | His slot | His pivot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| questionable A starter | A | earliest game | the fixed A slot: B fills the flex, and in the flex he would leave the A slot with nobody to fill it | none (clause 3: no A body on the bench, and the flex holder is not his position); the no-cover call, below |
| healthy B starter | B | last game | the FLEX (clause 2: the later kickoff of the two healthy B starters) | the C body into the flex, good until the last game kicks off |
| healthy B starter | B | late afternoon | the fixed B slot | the chain (clause 3): the flex holder shares his position and is still unlocked when his news would land, so the flex B starter into the fixed B slot and the C body into the flex, good until the late-afternoon window kicks off (his own lock) |
| bench C body | C | last game | the bench, named for both B starters: he covers whichever is ruled out first, and neither is flagged | |

The A starter's no-cover call. The free-agent pool is checked first: a pool A body whose game
has not started is a free add wherever a bench slot can be freed without losing a player who
matters (skill section 8's replaceability test), and once on the bench he is the pivot under
clause 3, good until the earliest game kicks off. Where the pool holds no such body, or the
only drop is the C body, the call is made before the earliest game kicks off, which is the
EARLIER of the questionable starter's kickoff and any safe alternative's: start him and
accept that there is no pivot, or start the safe alternative, said out loud. Had he played
the late-afternoon window with a bench A body in the earliest game, that body would be no
pivot, since he locks before the news lands, but he would be the safe alternative, and the
deadline would be the earliest game's kickoff, not the starter's own.

**(c) The chain: a surplus position flagged after the arrangement.** Two A starters, both
healthy when the lineup was arranged, so clause 2 gave the flex to the later kickoff: one in
the fixed A slot kicking off in the early window, one in the flex kicking off in the
late-afternoon window. A healthy B starter in the fixed B slot, early window. A bench C body
in the last game. No other A or B body on the roster. The fixed-slot A starter is then
scratched with no earlier flag, read off the inactive list about ninety minutes before the
early window.

| Player | Position | His window | His slot | His pivot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scratched A starter | A | early | the fixed A slot | the chain (clause 3): the flex A starter into the fixed A slot and the C body into the flex, two moves, good until the early window kicks off (his own lock, before the flex holder's) |
| healthy A starter | A | late afternoon | the FLEX (clause 2) | the C body into the flex, good until the late-afternoon window kicks off (his own lock), unless the chain has already spent him: the C body is named for both A risks |
| healthy B starter | B | early | the fixed B slot | none: no B body on the bench, and the flex holder is not his position; not flagged, so no call is due |
| bench C body | C | last game | the bench, named for both A starters: he covers whichever is ruled out first | |

The moment a flag lands while both A starters are unlocked, the arrangement is redone under
clause 1, since moving a starter between eligible slots is free: the questionable one into
the flex, the healthy one into the fixed A slot. His scratch then becomes one move, the C
body into the flex, good until his own early kickoff, and it no longer depends on the
healthy A starter being unlocked or on a second move landing in time. The chain is the
cover only when the arrangement cannot be redone: a scratch with no earlier flag, as here,
or the tie-break case, where both A starters are questionable and the earlier one holds the
fixed slot.

## Bench coverage by window

- A pivot is a pivot only if his game kicks off NO EARLIER than the moment the news arrives.
  For every questionable or fragile starter, name the pivot and the window it is good until.
- A bench body in the last game of the week is insurance for every earlier slot, directly
  through the flex or through the chain above. A bench body in the earliest game covers
  nobody but himself.
- When no bench body can cover a starter, check the free-agent pool first: a free agent
  whose game has not started is a free add (skill section 8's date rule), so he is the pivot
  wherever a slot can be freed without losing a player who matters. A starter no bench body
  or free agent covers takes the no-cover call, settled before the EARLIER of his own kickoff
  and the safe alternative's kickoff, because deferring past that lock chooses the
  questionable starter with no pivot. Said out loud, either way: start the safe player, or
  start the questionable one and accept that there is no pivot. It is the user's call, and it
  is a call, not a default.
- One bench body named as the pivot for two risks is said as such. He covers whichever of
  them is ruled out first, and the other is then uncovered: say which risk he is being kept
  for, or say that the second one has no pivot.
- The free-agent pool is a pivot source until each pool player's own kickoff. On a full bench
  that costs a drop (skill section 8's replaceability test), and where the host runs weekly
  waivers a pool player whose game has started is a claim, not an add.

## The game-day pass

A game-day pass is due on every practice-report day, on lock morning, and any time the user
asks about a lineup or about a player's status. It reads ALL four sources below, in this
order, for every league, and the answer NAMES what it read.

1. **The engine.** The Team Dashboard, re-synced first: its suggested lineup, its matchup
   column, its Suggested Moves box, and its Injury Tracker, with each flagged player's
   practice participation by day and his likelihood of playing. The matchup column is read
   and kept for every starter and for every name in a close call, so the user's question
   about a matchup is answered from what the pass already read. The day's practice report
   itself is read beside the tracker, since the tracker's column is a summary of it. Then
   the engine's news feed (Shark Bites) filtered to the user's rostered players, every item
   read with its timestamp. For any call inside a close band, the Injury Predictor row and
   Who Should I Start. A close band is two players within about a point on the engine's raw
   projection or on the host's projection, or the two services ordering them differently.
2. **The host.** Each roster row's status tag and its kickoff day and time; the host's
   projections on the roster page; the host's player notes; the host's game-day decision
   view where it exists, which carries the projected maximum and minimum, the start rate,
   and the sharp-user start rate where the host publishes one; and the matchup's two
   projected totals, which set the posture, floor when favored and ceiling when the
   underdog.
3. **The knowledgebase** (skill section 10): the availability list, the successor rows, and
   the week's schedule table.
4. **Dated web news, LAST**, and only for what none of the three above has posted yet. Every
   item carries its date. An undated item is dropped, and so is one older than what the
   tools already show.

**The betting line, on every close running back or receiver call.** For a close call where
EITHER player is a running back or a receiver, the game's point spread and total are read for
BOTH players' games, from a same-day dated odds page under source 4's rule, and each team's
implied total is stated beside the projections, the spread taken as a POSITIVE number of
points, since an odds page prints the favorite's as a negative number: for the favorite, half
the game's total plus half the spread; for the underdog, half the game's total minus half the
spread, so the favorite's implied total is always the higher of the two. Where a book
posts them, that player's own yardage and touchdown props are read too. A prop that cannot
be found is said as not found, never inferred, and a game with no posted line found is said
the same way, with the implied-total input silent on that side. The line informs game script
and touchdown chance, which is where a back's or a receiver's ceiling comes from, so it is
read BEFORE the posture rule's ceiling-or-floor argument is made: a lead back on a favored
team with the higher implied total holds the ceiling case over a committee back on an
underdog with a low total, whatever the ceiling column says. When the two players are on the
SAME TEAM the implied totals are identical and that input is silent; the game's total still
says how much scoring there is to share, and the props and the roles still separate them.
Two players on OPPOSING teams of one game need nothing special: the formula gives them
different implied totals, as it does for any two games. The line is a data point (skill
section 3), never a tie-breaker on its own: it joins the
inputs in the last rule below. It is required whenever either player in the close call is a
running back or a receiver, and permitted at tight end, at quarterback and in any other close
call where game script moves the number, never required there.

**Every starting slot, kicker and team defense included.** A pass covers every starting slot
in every league, not only the skill positions. For those two positions it pulls the host's
free-agent pool at the position, sorted by the week's projection, and compares the top
unlocked pool players to the rostered starter on BOTH numbers, the host's projection and the
engine's, which is the Free Agent Finder's projection for the WEEK BEING PLAYED: the Finder
carries a column for the current week and a separate one for the following week, and on a
game day the following week's column is never the number read. On waiver eve that same week
is the next week skill section 8's waiver-eve bullet already names for streamers.

- Both numbers higher than the rostered starter's: the swap is the call. The add's cost is
  read from the host's own row (skill section 8's cost rule): a free agent is free and
  instant, and a pool player on waivers is a claim, its cost and its arrival date named,
  which is rarely worth it at these positions. The rostered unit is usually replaceable under
  that section's test, the pool's depth at that tier checked first, so the drop usually costs
  little.
- One service higher only: a lean, said as one source, since one source is never a pass.
- The deadline is the EARLIER of the two kickoffs, the pool player's and the rostered
  unit's, exactly as any other pivot window. Usually that is the rostered unit's own kickoff,
  since that is when it locks. When the pool player's game starts first, HIS kickoff is the
  deadline, and where the host's weekly-waiver setting puts unclaimed players on waivers at
  kickoff he is a claim after it and not an add (the lock table above).
- A rostered kicker or team defense whose own game has already kicked off is locked: nothing
  can be done at that slot this week, and the pass says so rather than naming an upgrade the
  user cannot make.

Four rules hold the pass together.

- **One source is never a pass.** No status, no start and no sit is ever called from a single
  source.
- **A status is never called from an article when the host's roster row or the engine's
  tracker shows it.** Those two are read first and they are the current truth; an article can
  only add what they have not posted.
- **The answer says what it read**, in one line: the sources it reached and the ones it could
  not, so the user can see the pass was complete.
- **One engine number never outvotes the rest.** When the engine's composite value (3D)
  favors one player for a slot and the engine's raw projection, or the host's projection,
  favors the other, the call is made by five things together, the engine's raw projection,
  the host's projection and start rates, the betting line's implied totals and props (above,
  required at running back and receiver), the posture rule, and the lock order's optionality,
  a player who locks later keeping the slot changeable while a player who locks now takes the
  option with him; and the disagreement is said to the user in one line. The engine's
  composite is one data point (skill section 3), never the tie-breaker.

## Carried conditionals

A pass that defers a call on a named future event SETS a conditional. It is the same thing
this page already calls a pre-decided pivot or swap, written out in full: the condition (a
window's inactive list, a designation, a practice report, a report on a workload), the move
if it is met, the move if it is not, the deadline, which is the window that move is good
until under the pivot-window rule above, the EARLIER of the incoming player's kickoff and the
kickoff of the player leaving the slot, and the projections the call was made on as they read
at that moment, the beneficiary's and the absent or doubtful player's, the engine's number
and the host's for each. A pivot named for a questionable starter IS a carried
conditional, so every pivot the lock order names for a flagged starter is one. It is stated
in full to the user in the pass that sets it and written to the private document (skill
section 9), condition, both moves, deadline and those set-time numbers together; a
conditional the copilot holds only in its head is not set.

**Every pass that follows one which set a conditional opens with them.** That is every pass
after the first one on a game day, and every pass on a later day too, the first pass of a
game day included. Before any OTHER read, the pass reads the status each conditional set
earlier turns on and marks each one met, not met or still open, that status taken from the
host's roster row or the engine's tracker when either shows it, never from an article in
that case (the rule above), while a condition neither of them shows, a
practice report or a report on a workload, is marked from the dated report itself under
source 4's rule. Three cases, each said out loud:

- **Met.** The stated move IS the call, and it is said first, ahead of any new number: the
  condition, that it is met, and the move now being made. The comparison that produced the
  conditional is not re-run to decide it a second time.
- **Not met.** The stated alternative is the call, said in the same shape: the condition,
  that it is not met, and the alternative move now being made, or the lineup left as it is
  where the alternative was to leave it alone.
- **Still open.** It is carried forward with its deadline restated, naming what will resolve
  it and when. A conditional still open when its deadline arrives leaves the slot as it is
  locked, which resolves it to the stated alternative only where that alternative was to
  leave the lineup alone, and it is why a conditional whose resolving event lands AFTER its
  deadline cannot be carried at all: that one is the no-cover call (bench coverage, above),
  made by the user now, before the deadline.

**A reversal is named as a reversal.** The move of a met conditional is reversed only on
evidence that did not exist, or had not been read, when the conditional was set: a later
report of a snap cap or a workload limit on the incoming player, a new designation, a weather
turn at that scale. The evidence is named, the user is told that this is a REVERSAL of the
call already given, and both happen in the same message and before the deadline.
Re-running the comparison the conditional was set on is not new evidence, and neither is a
preference.

**A projection that already priced the news cannot argue the news does not matter.** When the
condition is confirmed, the beneficiary's projection is read against the ABSENT player's own
projection as it stood WHEN the conditional was set, not as it stands after the confirmation,
in three cases. Where those set-time numbers were not kept, the test cannot be run: the pass
says so, and the met move stands, which is the default anyway, since a met move is reversed
only on new evidence.

- **(a) The beneficiary's projection moved after the confirmation.** A move in either
  direction is new information, worth the size of the move, and it enters the comparison as
  such; a move DOWN is evidence the reversal rule above can use, and it is named as such.
- **(b) It did not move, and the absent player was already projected near zero.** The engine
  had priced the absence before the confirmation, so the beneficiary's number was the
  with-absence number all along and its not moving is evidence of nothing. The comparison is
  the same one the conditional was set on, so it is no ground for reversal.
- **(c) It did not move, and the absent player still carries a full projection.** The engine
  has not updated; the pass says "stale" out loud, and a number that has not caught up is no
  ground for reversal either.

In all three the projection stays a data point in the comparison (skill section 3). What it
cannot do is stand as evidence that the confirmed news changed nothing.

## The week

| When | What is read | What comes out of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First practice-report day | the game-day pass (above), then: each dashboard player's floor, projection, ceiling and 3D, scored to the synced league; the host's own projections and lineup suggestions as data points, and only where the tool's own settings panel shows them scored to this league (skill section 5's settings test), while its start and roster rates read the room, not the player, and survive that test (skill section 4); and the matchup rating per player | the week's draft lineup, its slots arranged by the lock order, and the questionable list opened |
| The middle practice days | the practice trend for each questionable starter, no participation to limited to full, plus the day's news | the pivot per risk, named with the window it is good until |
| The last practice-report day | the week's designations, which are the headline: out is out, doubtful is treated as out, questionable plays at variable rates and needs a pivot | starters confirmed or swapped, the arrangement re-checked against the windows |
| Waiver eve and the claim run | skill section 8's waiver cadence, read here for bodies that cover the open windows | the bench the pivots come from |
| The night before the week's first game | the two projected totals on the matchup; the host's pool at kicker and team defense by the week's projection, beside the rostered ones and the engine's numbers for both | the posture, floor when favored and ceiling when the underdog; every swap written down in advance as a carried conditional; the kicker and team-defense stream calls, each made before the EARLIER of the two kickoffs |
| About ninety minutes before each kickoff window, every game day | every conditional set earlier, marked met, not met or still open from that window's inactive list on the host's roster page and the day's injury tags, BEFORE any other read | the met conditionals' moves made and said first, the pre-decided swap made, then the host's team page re-read before the lineup is called set |

## Common mistakes

- Starting a name on reputation instead of on projection and usage.
- Overreacting to one week, in either direction.
- Ignoring the game script: a team likely to trail throws more, which lifts its pass catchers
  and thins its runner's floor.
- Starting a questionable player with no pre-decided pivot.
- Overweighting weather. Only extreme wind or heavy precipitation moves a projection far
  enough to change a start.
- A healthy early-week starter parked in the flex.
- Comparing more than three players for one slot. Past three, take the dashboard's ordering
  and stop.
- Calling a player's status from an article when the host's roster row or the engine's
  tracker already shows it, and letting one engine number outvote the host's tool, the
  posture and the lock order.
- Re-litigating a conditional the pass has just marked met by re-running the same comparison
  it was set on, instead of opening from the conditional and making the stated move.
- Reading an unmoved projection as "the news changed nothing" when the engine had already
  priced the absence, so that number was the with-absence number before the news confirmed
  it.
- Making a close running back or receiver call without the game's spread, total and implied
  team totals.
- Setting every running back, receiver and tight end slot and never looking at kicker and
  team defense, where a free pool upgrade both services rate higher sits unclaimed.

## Who clicks

Prime directive 1 holds all week: the user clicks every lineup change. The copilot names the
slot for each starter and the pivot for each risk, and re-reads the host's own team page
before saying any lineup is set.
