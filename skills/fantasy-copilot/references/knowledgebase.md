# The knowledgebase: template, build, refresh, search

Read this when building or refreshing the knowledgebase described in section 10 of the
skill. It is not needed at the clock; at the clock the files are searched, not this page.
## Where it lives

Beside the user's private document, under the user's control, never inside this skill:

    kb/<season>/
      README.md                       structure, vocabulary, refresh procedure
      refresh-log.md                  one line per refresh: date, trigger, what changed
      data/                           the source tables, saved as pulled, one file per table
      teams/<CODE>.md                 one dossier per NFL team: the rooms table, rookies, quotes, media read, watch
      coach/<CODE>-offense.md         play caller: six tendency rows, roster fit table, quotes
      coach/<CODE>-defense.md         coordinator: gives-up-by-position table, key defenders
      oline/<CODE>.md                 line: header numbers, starters table, impact, watch
      nfl/schedule.md                 the season schedule, every team, every week (hand-built once)
      nfl/defense-ratings.md          each defense rated soft/average/tough per position
      nfl/defense-injuries.md         key defenders out or limited, what each one softens
      schedule/<CODE>.md              the team's weekly matchup table by position (script)
      nfl/schedule-strength.md        softest and toughest by position (script)
      nfl/*.md, the rest              GENERATED by the rollups script from the team tables, never edited:
                                      injuries-and-designations, successor-map, rookies-and-young-players,
                                      hype-and-reports, suspensions-and-legal, coaching-and-scheme,
                                      coach-tendencies, defense-tendencies, offensive-lines
      build/                          the scripts and the workflow files the build and refresh run

"League" in these files means the NFL; the user's fantasy league is never mixed in.

Every profile is a set of tables in a fixed shape, because the NFL-wide files are joins
over them and the refresh writes cells rather than prose. The shape is enforced by a
script (`kb-lint.py`) that the build and every refresh stop on. Nothing in a profile
narrates how it was made: no source lists, no gap notes, no method notes. A fact carries
its outlet and date inline, in a few words.

## The templates

One file per team in each set, headings and table headers exactly as written, a cell
with no fact holding `-` and never a note about why.

**teams/<CODE>.md**

    # <CODE> <Team name> - refreshed <YYYY-MM-DD>
    Play caller: <name> (NEW 2026 | returning; first-time caller yes/no) | Head coach: <name> | Defensive coordinator: <name> (NEW | returning)
    ## Rooms
    | Player | Pos | Age | Role | Status | If out | Preseason usage and rest | Direction |
    ## Rookies and young players behind veterans
    | Player | Pos | Veteran ahead | Vet age | Vet games missed 2023-25 | Evidence | Takeover verdict |
    ## Coach statements
    - <YYYY-MM-DD> <speaker>: "<at most 12 words verbatim>" - paraphrase (outlet)
    ## Media read (last 14 days)
    | Player | Tag | Outlets | Line |
    ## Watch
    - <player>: the role change and the gap against the engine's number, with date

Rooms rows: QB1-QB2, RB1-RB4, WR1-WR5, TE1-TE2, in that order. Role = starter, RB1A,
committee, slot, TE2 and the like. Status = healthy or the designation with the injury,
the timeline and (outlet date). If out = PRIMARY <name> | COMMITTEE <names> | UNSETTLED
with the speaker or outlet and date, written once and nowhere else in the file; `-` for
depth players. Preseason usage and rest = snap or touch facts and whether he was rested
in the finale. Direction = bounceback | step-forward | downgrade | unchanged, copied
from the play caller's roster-fit table. Rookies: Evidence is a coach or media line
with (outlet date), never a depth chart alone; verdict = taking the job now | share
rising | blocked | too early. Media read: tag = sleeper | breakout | bust | risk |
handcuff | hype, outlets = count, line = one sentence with (outlet date).

**coach/<CODE>-offense.md**

    # <CODE> offense staff - refreshed <YYYY-MM-DD>
    Play caller: <name> (NEW | returning; prior stops: <team seasons>, ...) | Head coach: <name> | OC if different: <name>
    ## Tendencies
    | Tendency | Direction | Number (season, team, source) |
    ## Roster fit
    | Player | Pos | Direction | Driver |
    ## What he has said
    - <YYYY-MM-DD> <speaker>: "<at most 12 words>" - paraphrase (outlet)

Tendencies has exactly six rows: RB concentration, RB targets, WR1 share, TE share, Pass
rate vs league, Red zone. Direction is one phrase (one back carries it, committee,
TE-heavy, spreads targets, above league pass rate, runs inside the 10); one supporting
number per row with its season and team, from a saved table where one holds it. Roster
fit lists every fantasy-relevant player with the tendency number or the quote that
drives his direction. Optional and never chased: pass rate over expectation, tempo,
personnel groupings, slot versus outside, designed runs, deep-ball rate; written only
when a pulled table happens to hold one, as part of a row's number, never as a row of
its own.

**coach/<CODE>-defense.md**

    # <CODE> defense - refreshed <YYYY-MM-DD>
    Coordinator: <name> (NEW | returning; prior stops: ...) | Scheme: <one line> | 2025 blitz rate <n%>, pressure rate <n%> (source)
    ## Gives up by position (2025 basis)
    | Position | Points allowed per game | Rank (1 = softest) | Note |
    ## Key defenders
    | Player | Pos | Status | Absence softens |
    ## DEF/ST direction

Four gives-up rows (QB, RB, WR, TE) from the points-allowed table with the filled marker;
Note is the mechanism in a few words. Key defenders: edge, interior, linebacker, top two
corners, nickel, safeties, each with what his absence softens (QB, RB, WR, TE). The
defensive-injuries file and the weekly ratings adjustments are read from this table.

**oline/<CODE>.md**

    # <CODE> offensive line - refreshed <YYYY-MM-DD>
    Rank: <n> of 32 (DS <season>) | ESPN pass block win rate <n%> (<rank>), run block win rate <n%> (<rank>) | sacks allowed <n>, sack rate <n%> | RB1 yards before contact <n> per attempt | QB pressure rate <n%>
    ## Starters
    | Slot | Player | Note |
    ## Impact
    - RB1: / - QB: / - Pass-catchers: one line each
    ## Watch
    - the line change this week and who it affects, with date; "-" when none

Starters: LT, LG, C, RG, RT, then one Depth row (swing tackle, top interior backup).
Note = new | returning | the injury and status with (outlet date). Component grades,
lineman biographies and contract terms are never collected.

## Schedule strength by position

The schedule file holds every team's opponent for every week, built by two
independent fetches and reconciled. The defense-ratings file rates every defense
soft, average or tough per position (QB, RB, WR, TE), preseason from last season's
fantasy points allowed by position adjusted by the defensive profile's 2026 changes,
and in season from actual points allowed once four weeks are in (blended before
that), then adjusted for the week by the defensive-injuries file: every key defender
out, doubtful or newly on IR, with the position group his absence softens, so that a
defense's rating for THIS week can differ from its season rating and the difference is
the weakness to exploit. Each team's schedule file applies the ratings week by week and
counts the soft/average/tough matchups per position for weeks 1-4, mid-season, and
the playoff windows (weeks 15-17 for most leagues, 16-17 for four-team brackets); the
NFL-wide file lists the softest and toughest schedules by position for the season and
for each window, and carries the week-by-week grid. The team files and the NFL-wide
file are generated, not written: `scripts/schedule-tables.py` joins the schedule file
to the ratings file (Python 3.8 or later, no other dependency), so they are never
hand-edited and never produced by an agent. The script reads its team codes from
`data/teams.md`, the same file the validator reads, and stops with a message when the
file is missing; it carries no team of its own, and the rollup lists the codes in sorted
order. The script ships in the plugin's `scripts/` folder, beside the validator; copy it into `kb/<season>/` at the first
build, again after every plugin update (the plugin's copy is the source when the two
differ), and run it from there, `python3 schedule-tables.py --dir . --date <today>`,
adding `--week <coming week>` in season. If no Python is available, say so, report
the tables as not built, and leave them absent; an agent never writes them instead.
Both inputs keep the shapes the script reads. The schedule file: one table row per
team, the team code in the first cell, then exactly 18 cells, one per week, each
`@CODE`, `vs CODE` or `BYE` (one BYE per row), with any notes under a `## Notes`
heading. The ratings file: the team code alone in the Defense cell, one of the words
soft, average, tough in each rating cell, and the week's injury adjustments as a table
"Defense | Position | Season | Week N | Player out" under "## Week N injury
adjustments". The script stops with a message on any row it cannot read; it never
guesses. This is the weekly matchup input
and the season-long difficulty read for any player, read alongside the engine's
projection, not ahead of it; the Draft Sharks Strength of Schedule and Fantasy Points
Allowed tools are the overlay, read live for the week's numbers, and when they disagree
with the file the tool wins and the file gets a refresh line.
## Vocabulary

Status uses the host's words: healthy, questionable, doubtful, out, IR, IR-designated
to return, PUP, NFI, commissioner exempt, suspended (n games). A designation is a fact
with a date. "Expected to miss weeks" is a report and carries its outlet. A replacement
plan is PRIMARY (one man takes the whole role), COMMITTEE (a named split) or UNSETTLED,
and the cell says which source made it so. Hype is labeled hype; reporting is labeled
reporting. Two marker families exist and no others: `[filled <date>: <source>]` on a
number copied from a saved table (the source is the file under `data/` with its
extension, or several files comma-separated) or from a dated page (the outlet and its
date), and `[unverified <date>]` inside a cell that a single source supports and
nothing confirms. The check script validates the filled form; the lint only requires the
shape.
A cell with no fact holds `-`. A fact nobody will ever publish (the never-collected list
under Source tables) is not a cell at all. The lint fails a file that carries any other
bracket tag, a Sources or Gaps section, a URL, or a sentence about what a pass searched.

The check script (`check-fills.py`) holds every filled number to the table it cites: the
citation must resolve to a file under `data/`, the cell's row must name a player or team
the table carries (the player from the row's first cell, the team from the file name), and
at least one number in the cell must sit in that player's or team's rows. Write the number
as the table writes it, sign included. A computed figure (a share, a per-game average, a
rank you counted from a column) carries no filled marker: it gets a parenthetical naming
the table it was computed from, because the check reads a table cell on its own and a
rank is in no row. A team spelling is matched
longest-first from `data/teams.md`, so a short spelling never claims another club's row.
One correct figure carries a cell, so the check thins wrong figures out of a set without
clearing any one cell; that is what it proves and all it proves.

## Source hierarchy for roles and usage

Strongest first. A coach's or general manager's statement, with the speaker, the date
and the outlet, quoted at most a dozen words verbatim and then paraphrased. Preseason
usage: snap and touch counts, first-team reps, and rest, because a young player held
out of the finale after playing earlier is a player the staff has decided on. Beat
reporting from the last two weeks. Themed article sweeps seeded with the season's
sleeper, breakout, bust, risky-pick and handcuff pieces from the major outlets and from
Draft Sharks' own advice pages, because the same name in three outlets says something
about what a draft room will do. Depth charts last: a depth chart is a list, not a
plan, and it says nothing about share. This hierarchy is for roles and usage only. For
value, projections and injury timelines, Draft Sharks stays the primary source (skill
section 6, step 9): its Injury Predictor, Shark Bites and rest-of-season projections
supply the numbers the dossier records next to the role.
## Source tables: pull the numbers before the profiles ask for them

A profile that needs a number never sends an agent to find it. Every number a template
row asks for comes from a table pulled once into `data/`, saved as read with its URL,
pull date, season, row floor and column order in the header. `pull-list.py` is the list:
twenty tables and nothing else, each with its cadence (weekly in season, once a season,
or the prior two seasons once) and the columns kept at pull time, and `--check` refuses
a build or a refresh until the files a run needs are present. Trim at the pull: a player
table is cut at a stated points or attempts floor, a wide page to the columns a template
row uses; width that never lands in the folder never costs a token again.

The kept twenty, by what they answer: the team codes and spellings; Draft Sharks points
allowed by position (the ratings basis and the defense gives-up rows), Draft Sharks
historical stats for RB, WR and TE (opportunity and target shares, red-zone and end-zone
opportunities), the Draft Sharks line rankings article; the Pro Football Reference
advanced passing and rushing pages (pressure, pocket time, blitzes, scrambles, play-action
volume; yards before contact), the team advanced page, the three red zone pages
(rushing, receiving and passing, each with inside-20 and inside-10 splits and rushing
with inside-5), the fantasy rankings page per
season for the last three (age, games played and started), the season index and
opposition pages per season for the last three (team plays, sacks taken, blitz, hurry and
pressure rates); the ESPN win-rate leaderboard; Sharp Football's coverage scheme rates;
rbsdm's neutral pass rate table (cited by the span its filter actually covers); Next Gen
Stats passing leaders; TeamRankings pace and red-zone pages. Draft Sharks tables are read
as a subscriber in the browser; the rest are free pages that a fetch tool cannot reach
(bot checks, JS apps) and the browser can: navigate, run a page script that rewrites the
document body as one pipe-separated line per row from the chosen columns, read the page
text, save it under the header. A script that returns the table as its result is capped
at about a kilobyte, a clipboard write hangs, and a request from the page to a local
receiver is blocked, so none of those is retried.

Never collected, and never carried in a profile as a cell: a figure only a subscription
publishes (PFF grades beyond what an article quotes, FTN line yards and DVOA, Fantasy
Points Data, Sharp's book-only splits, paid Sumer Sports and Next Gen Stats tiers:
personnel groupings, alignment and route rates, tempo and no-huddle rates, pass rate over
expectation, designed-run splits, most deep-ball rates) unless the user holds that
subscription and says so; a figure no outlet prints (camp rep counts, preseason snap
counts and shares); and the fat the 2026 audit named, which no decision reads: contract
dollars and cap figures, birth dates, season box-score lines the engine's projection is
built on, practice-squad and cut lists, position-coach rosters, kickoff logistics and
travel, ownership and front-office legal items, per-season tendency splits beyond the six
rows, per-defender advanced pages, lineman biographies and component grades, and any
season more than three back. A pass that meets one of these writes nothing and records
nothing; a profile carries facts a decision reads and cells a later refresh can fill.

## Build (once, before the season)

Two sessions at most (the search quota, not tokens, sets the split), about twenty-five
agents, most on the extraction tier; no cross-cut agents, no critic, no synthesis, no
gap rounds. Confirm the location with the user and get an explicit go-ahead before the
first build (skill section 10).

0. `data/teams.md` first. Then the pull list: `python3 pull-list.py --dir . --annual`
   names every table; the browser pulls them with trimmed columns; `--check` must pass
   before anything else runs. The season schedule is fetched twice and reconciled into
   `nfl/schedule.md` by hand, once.
1. Scaffold (`build-scaffold.py --dir . --date <today> --season <season>`, seconds): every
   per-team file is written in its template shape, with the team-level cells a saved table
   holds already filled and stamped (the four gives-up rows from the points-allowed table,
   the ESPN win rates and the sacks allowed in the line header). What is left empty is the
   judgment: rooms, roles, statuses, absence plans, quotes, rest, directions, verdicts, the
   six tendency numbers. After step 2 has named the players, `build-scaffold.py --ages`
   fills each `-` Age cell and each rookie row's veteran age and games missed from the
   fantasy pages by exact name, counting only the seasons the pages hold and saying how
   many that is (a veteran below the pull floor in an earlier season is not a veteran who
   missed that season), so the "how old, how injury-prone" half of the rookies table is a
   copy. The scaffold indexes the saved tables by the column layouts the pull list names;
   a table trimmed to other columns yields `-`, never a figure from the wrong column.
2. One judgment agent per team (session model, fetch first from three seed pages: the
   team site, a wire team page, one beat outlet; at most three searches) fills the
   judgment cells of all four files from the source hierarchy, with the vocabulary above,
   inventing nothing and writing `-` where nothing dated exists.
3. One skeptic per division (session model, fetch first, at most three searches) re-checks
   the high-impact cells of EVERY team in its division, never a sample: starters'
   designations and timelines, absence plans, the caller and coordinator in the header,
   preseason rest, key defenders, line starters and the line rank; corrects in place or
   marks `[unverified <date>]`. The same pass, against the previous copy, closes any
   rewrite or reshape of existing profiles: on the first reshape a lower-tier rewrite
   dropped about thirteen required facts per team (428 over 32 teams); a sample of two
   teams in each of the eight divisions found 152 of them, and only the pass over the
   other sixteen teams found the remaining 276.
4. Scripts: `kb-lint.py` (must pass), `check-fills.py --date all` (must exit clean),
   `rollups.py` (writes every NFL-wide file from the tables), the defense ratings file
   from the points-allowed table with the profiles' notes, `schedule-tables.py` (the 32
   matchup tables and the rollup), and the README and the first refresh-log line.

There is no gap round. A cell the judgment agent could not fill holds `-` and the next
refresh's collectors are seeded from those cells (`refresh-seed.py` prints the starters
whose absence plan is still open). Progress is the count of `-` cells in the required
columns, which the lint prints on its last line, never a marker count; the lint fails a
skeleton on its row minimums until step 2 has filled the rooms, which is the point. The one lesson from the first build
that stays: every agent gets the template, the vocabulary and the rules verbatim in its
prompt, and a five-team pilot runs before the pool after any prompt change.

## Refresh (a delta)

Weekly, the morning after the week's last game and before waivers clear; and before
every draft, lineup lock, trade decision, waiver or free-agent move, and roster move.
One session, about twenty agents, under sixty searches, under an hour. The runnable
form is the season folder's `refresh.js`; the shape is:

0. Tables: `pull-list.py --week N` names the weekly set (the in-season pages the games
   change: advanced passing and rushing, team advanced, red zone, team offense and
   defense, Next Gen Stats, TeamRankings, the Draft Sharks points-allowed and usage
   tables); the browser pulls them; `--check` must pass. Annual articles are pulled once
   when they publish; prior seasons never again. A new season starts as the build does:
   `data/teams.md`, then the annual and prior-season pulls, then the weekly set as soon
   as a week is played, with last season's in-season tables kept under their season name.
1. Collect once, three agents: the injury wire (extraction tier, fetch first, every skill
   position, every line starter, every defensive starter, with practice participation in
   season), the transactions wire (extraction tier: IR, PUP, exempt, suspensions, signings,
   releases, trades, elevations, play-caller changes), and role news with coach statements
   (session model, at most twelve searches, spent first on the starters whose absence
   plan is still open per `refresh-seed.py` and on this week's new designations; it returns
   the PRIMARY / COMMITTEE / UNSETTLED call it judged from the coach's words, the quote,
   the rest or usage fact). Items are routed to teams in code.
2. Apply, one editor per division (extraction tier): each collected item goes into its
   cell by kind (status, plan, quote, rest or usage, transaction, caller, line note, key
   defender status), one row at a time, keeping every other cell and the column count;
   an item older than the fact in the cell is skipped. Each editor returns its high-impact
   changes: a starter's designation, an absence plan, a caller change, a line starter out.
3. Verify, one skeptic per division (session model, fetch first, at most three searches):
   the high-impact cells only; corrected in place, or `[unverified <date>]` inside the cell.
4. In season, one judgment agent: the defensive-injuries file from the collected defender
   statuses and the key-defender tables, then the ratings file (the 2026 points-allowed
   table replaces the 2025 basis once four weeks are in; blended until then) with the
   week's injury adjustments as the table the script reads.
5. Scripts, from the workflow's `next` field: `kb-lint.py`, `rollups.py`, and in season
   `schedule-tables.py --week N`. The usage check against each player's direction is a
   script over the pulled usage table once the directions carry a measurable form; until
   then the roles collector's usage items land in the rooms table and the direction is
   changed by the editor only on a second consecutive diverging week.
6. Overlay (the user's browser, no agent): for every absence plan settled and every caller
   change, read the engine's current value and rest-of-season projection and write the gap
   into the team's Watch list. Nothing is written back into the engine.
7. Log one line in refresh-log.md: date, trigger, items, cells changed, high-impact
   checked, plans settled, searches.

The pre-draft sweep in section 6 step 9 becomes: refresh, then search only for what is
newer than the refresh, within the step's own recency window, and read every target
against the successor map, the rookies file and the media read before it earns a band.

## Agents, models and scripts

Three kinds of work, and what each runs on:

- **Judgment**: a coach's words into PRIMARY or COMMITTEE, a rookie's evidence into a
  verdict, a player's direction under a caller, every skeptic. The session model, at its
  normal effort, and as few of them as the shape allows: one per team at the build, one
  per division at a refresh, one for the ratings in season.
- **Extraction**: a league-wide page into items with team codes, a collected item into
  its cell. A lower tier does this as well as the strongest one; the lint and the check
  script catch what it gets wrong in shape, the division skeptic what it gets wrong in
  substance.
- **Joins**: anything derivable from files that already exist: every NFL-wide rollup, the
  schedule tables, the lint, the fill check, the yardstick, the pull list, the refresh
  seed. A script, never a model: exact, free, seconds, re-runnable every week.

Two shapes follow. Collect once, then route: a fact that covers the whole NFL is read by
one collector and handed to the editors in their prompt. Edit only cells: the profiles
are tables so that a refresh writes a cell and a script writes the rollups, which is
what removes the per-file merge agent and the per-team skeptic that made the first
refresh design unrunnable inside one session's search quota. A workflow runs a handful
of agents at once (the harness caps concurrency by the machine's cores), so a pool of a
hundred file-level agents is split across two or three workflows launched together, each
with its own list; several judgment workflows at once still trip the API's rate limits.
A failed agent call is retried once under a distinct label and then counted as a gap in
the report, never filled in. The first run of any reshaped refresh logs its per-phase
agent counts and tokens from the workflow's return before anyone quotes a cost.

## Search, do not browse

Any OS, run from `kb/<season>/`. Examples with a Unix shell and with PowerShell:

    grep -n "PRIMARY" nfl/successor-map.md
    Select-String -Path nfl\successor-map.md -Pattern "PRIMARY"

    grep -n -A2 "<player>" teams/<CODE>.md
    Select-String -Path teams\<CODE>.md -Pattern "<player>" -Context 0,2

    grep -n "PUP\|IR-designated\|exempt" nfl/injuries-and-designations.md
    Select-String -Path nfl\injuries-and-designations.md -Pattern "PUP|IR-designated|exempt"

Who eats if a starter is out: the successor map. Whether a young player is taking the
job: the rookies file. Whether a designation allows an IR slot: the injuries-and-designations file's
Status column, then the host's own row, which wins on disagreement.
